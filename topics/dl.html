Deep learning

Twenty-five pages, in learning order: the neuron and its activations, how networks are trained (forward pass, backpropagation, losses, optimisers), how they are regularized, and the main architectures: CNNs, RNNs, LSTMs, GRUs, attention and Transformers, transfer learning, autoencoders, GANs and VAEs.

Why it matters: every modern AI system, from image recognition to language models, is built from these pieces. Knowing what each one computes, with real numbers, makes the big models far less mysterious.

    Which one to pick?

    flowchart TD
  A{"What is your data?"} -->|"images"| C["CNN + pooling (or transfer learning if data is small)"]
  A -->|"sequences: text, audio, time series"| S{"Long sequences, lots of data?"}
  S -->|yes| T["Transformer (attention)"]
  S -->|"no, small and simple"| R["LSTM / GRU"]
  A -->|"tables of features"| M["MLP (or gradient boosting)"]
  A -->|"want to generate new data"| G{"Sharp samples or a smooth latent space?"}
  G -->|sharp| GAN["GAN"]
  G -->|smooth latent space| VAE["VAE"]
  A -->|"unlabelled, want compression or anomalies"| AE["Autoencoder"]

    Key points

    • A network is layers of neurons because each layer computes $g(Wx + b)$ on the previous layer's output.
    • Training is gradient descent because backpropagation gives the gradient of the loss for every weight.
    • Adam is the usual default because it combines momentum with per-weight step sizes.
    • Dropout, batch norm and early stopping fight overfitting because each stops the network from memorising its training data.
    • Architectures match data shapes because CNNs exploit local image structure, RNNs and Transformers sequence order.

    Video talking points

    Use these 5 bullets as your script outline. Say each one in your own words. Do not read them: glance at them and speak.

    1. Hook (10 sec). Ask: how does a pile of simple weighted sums end up recognising faces and writing text?
    2. The big picture (30 sec). Neurons stack into layers; forward propagation predicts, backpropagation computes gradients, and an optimiser such as SGD or Adam updates the weights to lower the loss.
    3. Architecture follows the data (30 sec). Images use CNNs with shared filters; sequences use RNNs, LSTMs or GRUs, or attention; Transformers process all positions in parallel; autoencoders, VAEs and GANs generate.
    4. How to choose (45 sec). Walk the "Which one to pick?" flowchart: grid data, CNN; sequences, Transformer or LSTM; little labelled data, transfer learning; generating samples, VAE or GAN; overfitting, dropout, early stopping or batch normalization.
    5. One takeaway (10 sec). Every deep network is trained the same way: forward, loss, backward, step; only the architecture changes.
