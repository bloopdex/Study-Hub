Graphs

Eleven pages on graphs: how to store them, how to walk them (BFS, DFS), how to find shortest paths (Dijkstra, Bellman-Ford, Floyd-Warshall), how to connect everything cheaply (Kruskal, Prim), and how to order and check them (topological sort, cycle detection, Union-Find).

Why it matters: roads, networks, dependencies and social links are all graphs. Knowing which algorithm fits which question, and its precondition, is most of the work.

    Key points

    • Adjacency lists make traversals $O(V + E)$ because they store only real edges.
    • BFS gives shortest unweighted paths because it reaches vertices layer by layer.
    • Dijkstra needs non-negative weights because it declares distances final as soon as they are the smallest.
    • Bellman-Ford and Floyd-Warshall allow negative edges because they never finalise a distance early.
    • Kruskal and Prim are both greedy because the cheapest edge across any cut is always safe for a minimum spanning tree.
