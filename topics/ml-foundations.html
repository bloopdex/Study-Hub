ML foundations

Eight ideas every machine-learning project rests on: the learning paradigms, how to score classifiers and regressors, why models underfit or overfit, how regularization helps, how to evaluate honestly with cross-validation, how to prepare data, and how to avoid leakage.

Why it matters: most failed ML projects fail here, not in the choice of algorithm: a wrong metric, an overfit model, unscaled features or a leaky evaluation.

    Key points

    • The paradigm comes first because labels, or their absence, decide which methods are possible.
    • Metrics must match costs because a missed positive and a false alarm are rarely equally bad.
    • Training error is not enough because flexible models can memorise noise.
    • Cross-validation gives honest comparisons because every score comes from unseen data.
    • Everything is fitted on training data only because anything else leaks information into the evaluation.

    Video talking points

    Use these 5 bullets as your script outline. Say each one in your own words. Do not read them: glance at them and speak.

    1. Hook (10 sec). Ask: why do so many machine-learning models look great in the lab and fail in the real world?
    2. The big picture (30 sec). ML foundations are the rules that make a score trustworthy: the right paradigm, the right metric, honest validation, and no leakage.
    3. Bias vs variance (30 sec). Too simple a model underfits, too flexible a model overfits. Regularization and more data fight variance; a richer model fights bias.
    4. How to choose (45 sec). Walk the "Which one to pick?" flowchart: classes, use precision, recall and F1; numbers, use MAE, MSE or $R^2$; choosing settings, use cross-validation; overfitting, regularize; always preprocess inside the training data.
    5. One takeaway (10 sec). A model is only as good as the honesty of its evaluation.
