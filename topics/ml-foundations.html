    Key points

    • The paradigm comes first because labels, or their absence, decide which methods are possible.
    • Metrics must match costs because a missed positive and a false alarm are rarely equally bad.
    • Training error is not enough because flexible models can memorise noise.
    • Cross-validation gives honest comparisons because every score comes from unseen data.
    • Everything is fitted on training data only because anything else leaks information into the evaluation.
